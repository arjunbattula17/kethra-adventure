# Lore Bible

The single source for every piece of story text in the game. In-game lines are written from this
document, never the other way round. It expands what the team already wrote; the **Canon sources**
section at the end lists every existing file it builds on, so a writer can check any claim.

Rating: E for Everyone. Danger is weather, darkness, failing machines and distance. Nobody in this
setting fights anybody.

---

## The world

**Where.** A single star system on the far side of the **Kessic Drift**, a sparse region of space
that crews avoid because ships crossing it sometimes go quiet and are never heard from again. No
human chart shows this system. It has one star, an asteroid belt and four worlds:

| World | What it is | Seen from orbit |
|---|---|---|
| **Kethra** | First orbit past the belt. Terraced ruins under a bioluminescent canopy; home of the Aiveth. | green, with a faint night-side glow |
| **Vessek Anchorage** | A broken ring of stranded ships lashed to the remains of a Kindling ring-station. About 300 people live there. | violet, ringed, points of light that blink out of sync |
| **Orrun's Reach** | A storm desert. Its dunes shift to show, then bury again, Kindling machinery the size of towns. | rust, banded by storms |
| **Isilthe** | An ocean moon, the outermost world. Something under its sea transmits a signal on a fixed cycle. | blue, ringed |

**When.** The player's present is the 23rd day of a survey voyage, in a future where ships cross
between stars with a hyperdrive. Crossings take weeks. Freight and survey work is contracted by
companies like **ARK Ltd**, whose hulls and **Orion Interfaces** consoles fill the player's ship.

**Technology.** Humans: hyperdrives, deflector shields, cold-sleep berths for long hauls,
scanners that read light (spectroscopy, Doppler). Everything breaks and gets patched. The
**Kindling**, who built the ruins, worked with light and water: their machines move water up
through living structures and turn it into light, and they grew **resonant crystals** that hold
and tune colour. Nobody alive knows how any of it works.

**Daily life.**
- *Aboard the Wren:* watch rotations, the hum of life support, coffee that tastes of the recycler.
  One person awake on watch while the other two sleep in cold berths.
- *On Kethra:* the Aiveth live in the canopy. They tend terraces, carry lanterns and speak half in
  words, half in the glow along their arms: a held colour carries what a word can't. Once a
  season the whole grove performs the Rite of Three Breaths to thank the Cistern Heart.
- *At the Anchorage:* salvage shifts, rationed hydroponics, a school in a cargo hold. Children
  who have never seen a sky with other stars in it. Everyone knows the drill for a white sky:
  shut everything down and wait.

## History

Dates are counted in years before the Wren arrives (**BA**). Aiveth record-keeping counts
seasons; the Anchorage keeps a ledger from the day its first ship arrived.

1. **c. 1,150 BA: the Choir is finished.** The Kindling complete a transmitter under Isilthe's
   sea, Cistern Hearts on Kethra, and relay machines under Orrun's sand. Kindling carvings call the
   transmitter something like "the voice that calls home". The Anchorage later names it the
   **Choir**.
2. **c. 880 BA: the White Sky.** On a single day, every Kindling light answers the Choir at once.
   The sky over every terrace turns white, and the Kindling are gone. There was no war and no
   plague. The last carvings describe the same moment again and again, in different hands, and
   then nothing more is ever carved.
3. **c. 610 BA: the Aiveth climb.** The Aiveth move up from the lower boughs into the empty
   terraces "the way a bird inherits an empty nest". They learn the Rite of Three Breaths from the
   surviving carvings, then pass it down by ear, and small errors creep in.
4. **142 BA: the first castaways.** The ore hauler *Lantern Bay* is pulled across the Drift by a
   white sky and loses power in this system. Her crew moors to a dead Kindling ring above Vessek
   and survives. Another ship is pulled in every six to nine years; each one is lashed on. That is
   **Vessek Anchorage**.
5. **19 BA: the traders.** An Anchorage salvage crew lands on Kethra, wins the Aiveth's trust,
   and leaves with three cart-loads of grave goods from the lower terraces. The Aiveth close the
   landing terrace to outsiders. Warden Corvenna was an acolyte at the time.
6. **Three seasons BA: the Heart dims.** Kethra's Cistern Heart begins to dim, and gets dimmer
   every season. The lower boughs darken first.

**Present, days 1–23 of the voyage.** The survey freighter *Wren* leaves port on a contract from
ARK Ltd to find out what happens to ships in the Kessic Drift. Day 14: the charts keep solving
for a mass that isn't on any survey. Day 22: a repeating signal with a structure to it. Day 23:
light from every direction at once, and every system fails. The Wren is now in this system,
running on emergency power.

## The central conflict

**Who wants what.**
- **The player** wants to get the Wren flying and get home, and the crew with them.
- **The Aiveth** want their Cistern Heart lit, and outsiders to stop taking things.
- **The Anchorage** wants what it has wanted for 142 years: a way out. It lives on salvage, and
  a working hyperdrive core is worth more to it than anything the Wren could offer in trade.

**What stands in the way.**
- The Wren needs parts: **resonant crystals** (Kindling-grown, found on Kethra), **conduit
  alloy** (Anchorage salvage), and a **stellar core shard** (only Orrun's buried machines hold
  them).
- Nobody here trusts outsiders, for good reasons on both sides.
- The Choir still fires. No ship has ever left this system under power. The Anchorage has tried
  four times, and every attempt ended with a white sky and a dead drive.

**What happens if the player fails.** The Wren becomes the Anchorage's newest hull. The two
sleeping crew stay asleep on reserve power while it lasts. Kethra's lower boughs go dark, and Fen's
younger sibling Oshel sleeps through the daylight because there isn't enough of it left. And the
Choir keeps pulling in the next crew who come looking.

## Factions and notable figures

**The Aiveth of Kethra.** A canopy people who inherited the Kindling's city without the manual.
*Motive you can argue for:* the last outsiders robbed their dead, and the only thing keeping their
children's groves lit is a machine they don't understand. Guarding it is the rational choice.
- **Warden Corvenna** (kor-VEN-uh), gatekeeper of the landing terrace. Wary, exact, fair once
  proven. She privately believes the Rite has been broken for her whole life and tells no
  acolyte so. *Wants:* her people safe, and the truth about the Rite, in that order.
- **Fen Larkspur**, third-generation archive-keeper. Young, delighted by questions, impatient with
  elders. *Wants:* the Heart fixed rather than worshipped, because their sibling **Oshel**'s grove is
  the dimmest on the terrace.

**Vessek Anchorage.** About 300 descendants of stranded crews, living in a ring of wrecks.
Practical, generous with strangers, ruthless with salvage. *Motive you can argue for:* they have
kept children alive for five generations on scrap. The trip that robbed Kethra fed the Anchorage
through a bad year.
- **Harbormaster Ilse Varro**, who keeps the ledger and the peace. Born on the ring. *Wants:* one
  working ship to get a message home, and she will bargain hard for the Wren's core to get it.

**The Kindling** (the Aiveth's name for them; nobody knows what they called themselves). The
builders, gone for 880 years. They are not coming back to explain anything, and they were not
villains: every carving they left is about light, water and going home.

**The Choir** is a machine, not a character. It has no intentions. It is still doing what it
was built to do, on a schedule, with nobody left to answer it.

## The player

- **Who:** the Wren's survey lead: part archaeologist, part engineer, fully responsible. The
  character sheet calls them *Explorer*. No name, no gender, no face: the player fills it in.
- **Why here, now:** ARK Ltd paid for the voyage after three of its freighters went quiet in the
  Kessic Drift. A broker in port sold them a nav fragment recovered from the Drift's edge showing
  a star where there shouldn't be one. The player came to find out what happens to ships out
  here, and it happened to the Wren.
- **Knows at the start:** the ship is crippled, life support is up, navigation, hyperdrive and
  long-range scan are down. The two other crew are safe in cold sleep. There was a white light.
  Nothing outside matches any chart.
- **Does not know:** that other humans are here, that the white light has a source, that it has
  happened before (to the Kindling, and to the Anchorage every few years), or that fixing Kethra's
  Heart will make the problem louder.

## The goal

**One sentence, after the intro:** *Get the Wren flying again and find the way home.*

**The deeper objective (revealed mid-game, never in the intro):** the Cistern Hearts are relays
for the Choir. Waking Kethra's Heart, the right thing to do for the Aiveth, also strengthens the
signal that traps ships here. The way home is not escaping the Choir but reaching Isilthe and
**teaching it a different song**: retuning it so it keeps feeding the Hearts but stops calling
ships in. That takes the Kindling script (the Aiveth), salvage and a ship that can fly (the
Anchorage and the Wren), and the player's skills to join the two.

## Open questions (deliberately unanswered)

1. **Where did the Kindling go?** "Called home" is literal in their carvings. Home to where?
2. **Why is the Heart dimming now?** Three seasons ago something changed in the Choir's cycle.
   Wear? Or is the Choir calling harder?
3. **What is under Orrun's sand?** The relays are the size of towns. One may still have power.
4. **Who sold ARK Ltd the nav fragment**, and how did it get out of the Drift when no ship has?
5. **Does the Wickmoth belong to the Kindling's machines or to Kethra?** It predates the Aiveth.

### Conflicts in existing text, and the reading chosen
- **"Alone" vs. "the only one aboard awake."** The reveal caption says the player is "stranded,
  alone", the cold open says "the only one aboard awake", and the final log says "we were never
  supposed to—". *Chosen reading:* two crew are in cold sleep, so the player is the only one awake
  and alone in the sense that matters. This keeps all three lines true. **Team decision:** keep
  the sleepers, or make the player the only person aboard (then the log's "we" means the crew
  back in port).
- **"A galaxy no chart has ever mapped" vs. the course plot's exact distances.** *Chosen
  reading:* no human chart; the Wren measures this system live, which is what the course-plot
  puzzle is.
- **The Warden's "traders"** are never named in dialogue. *Chosen reading:* an Anchorage salvage
  crew. This connects Kethra's distrust to the Anchorage without changing a line.
